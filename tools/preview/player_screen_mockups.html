RankUp player screen: three options

Each mock-up is one Minecraft screen at the bigger size you asked for (decision D29): 520 by 300 GUI pixels, drawn at GUI scale 2, in BSP Core's Holo HUD style. The same player in all three, Mrgregles: a Warden (rank 3 of 5) with 2 140 lifetime points, 640 points this season, on the way to Vanguard at 4 000 (seasonal); 19 h 40 min played, 31 player kills, 212 mob kills, 6 totems stolen, 4 protected; the title [Warden]. The ladder is the one from the admin mock-ups. Pick one, or name the parts you want combined.

A. Ladder card the track down the left with your rung lit, your standing on the right

The ladder drawn as rungs on a track, every rung with its name and points, yours lit in gold, the ones below filled, seasonal ones tagged. On the right: your rank in large letters with your title, the points bar to the next rung, what the next rung gives, then every signal that counts with its count, a bar of its share and its points. One line says what would get you there fastest.

Good at: seeing where you are on the whole ladder at a glance and what to do next; the same drawing can serve the admin screen's LADDER tab later. Weak at: long ladders (more than seven rungs scroll); the right side is dense.

B. Progress sheet numbers first: the table of what counts, and what gets you there

A stats page. The header holds your rank, title, lifetime and season points, and one wide bar to the next rung. Below, a full table: every signal with its count, the points each is worth, the points it has given you, and its share. On the right, the next rung's rewards and three ways to reach it, then the rung after that, so the whole ladder reads as a list of goals.

Good at: players who want the arithmetic: how much each thing is worth and what to do; the easiest to build (text and bars, no drawing). Weak at: the ladder's shape is a list, not a picture; little to look at.

C. Journey the ladder as a path across the top, your marker between rungs, big tiles below

The rungs are nodes on a path from left to right, the path lit up to you and a gold marker standing between Warden and Vanguard as far along as your season points go, the next rung's rewards floating under it. Below, four big tiles with the numbers that matter most (time, kills, totems, season) and a feed of your latest gains ("+150 totem stolen from Kestrel, 2 h ago"), so the screen is also a log of what you did.

Good at: the feel of progress: the marker moving along, the feed of gains, few numbers; a good screen to leave open. Weak at: the full arithmetic (worth per signal) is not shown; the feed needs the ledger to keep recent changes (a small addition, kept in memory per session); the most to build.

Decisions, with defaults

Answer by number in chat; "default" takes the one in gold. The answers go in RankUp - Design Decisions.

  1. Layout. A ladder card, B progress sheet, C journey, or parts combined. Default: A, with B's "worth" column in the signal table.
  2. How it opens. Default: /rank opens the screen; /rank <player> for admins stays chat text; a key binding later if you want one.
  3. Which signals show. Every signal that is on (even at 0), with the off ones hidden; or everything the player has a count for. Default: every signal that is on; off signals with a count shown dimmed, as in B.
  4. How much of the ladder. The whole ladder with every rung's points and rewards, or only the next rung. Default: the whole ladder; a rung's rewards on hover.
  5. Other players' standing. How many hold each rung, who holds the top. Default: the count per rung (as in A), no names; a leaderboard later if wanted.
  6. "Fastest way there". The line that turns the points to go into counts per signal. Default: yes, the three biggest-weight signals that are on.
  7. Latest gains feed (C). Needs the ledger to remember a player's recent changes while they are online. Default: no for now; it can be added to any layout later.
  8. Refresh. Default: the server sends the standing when the screen opens and again every second while it is open.
  9. Size. Default: as the admin screen (D29): fills the window minus a margin, at least 428 by 240, text at the normal size.
